Concept v1

Levain

Bread on a busy schedule. One loaf or five, the app answers the same question — what needs doing next, and when — and helps you place a bake where your day actually has room for it. Recipes and starters live in the back.

Decisions I made from your answers
01
The clock anchors to reality, not the plan. Every step stores a duration, never a fixed hour. Tap a step done and everything downstream re-projects from that timestamp. Miss one and it turns rust-red, then re-projects when you finally tap.
02
Now is a stack, not a hero. You said one big next action, but five bakes means five. So: the most urgent one is the large card and the rest sit under it, compressed to a line each. It degrades gracefully at one bake and at seven.
03
A bake can carry several loaves. One dough splits into variants at shaping — the timeline stays single up to that point, then the shape step lists what goes into each. Separate doughs are just separate bakes.
04
Judgement steps get a window, not a time. Bulk and cold proof show a range and the cue to look for. I know you picked plain adaptive — this is the one place I pushed, because "4:10 PM" on a bulk ferment is a lie the app can't back up. Say the word and it collapses to a single time.
05
No equipment logic, but it does sequence your hands. Every step carries its hands-on minutes. The day view separates work from waiting, and when two actions land together the app names the better order and why. It never reschedules anything for you.
06
Five methods, not one. Sourdough, kneaded and enriched, bagels, pizza and discard bakes each get their own step template. The ordering advice only works because kneading is fifteen hands-on minutes and a bulk ferment is none.
07
Start times are judged against your sleep. Open a recipe and it offers candidate start times, flagging any that would put hands-on work between 9:30pm and 6am.
